AlphaVerify

An open-source framework for one question every trading signal should face: does its apparent edge hold up against markets that contain no signal at all?

1The question

Market folklore is full of conditions said to change the odds: a golden cross, price above its 200-day average, unusually low bond yields. Measured on history, many of them do appear to shift the probability of a rise or a fall. The question is whether that shift is an edge or something any market would produce by chance.

AlphaVerify answers it by running the same measurement on a thousand synthetic markets built to have no memory — markets in which, by construction, nothing in the past can predict the next day. A condition counts as an edge only if its real score beats 95% of the scores those signal-free markets produce.

2How it works

Each condition is split into ranges (“bins”), and each bin passes through five stages. Every stage writes its results to disk; selection consumes only a current validation result, and the forecast only a current selection.

StageCommandWhat it does
1. MeasuremeasureFor each bin, the probability that the market moves by a given percentage within a given number of days.
2. ComparecompareSubtract the unconditional market baseline, leaving the shift the condition is associated with.
3. ValidatevalidateScore the shift and repeat the whole measurement on 1,000 synthetic markets. The bin clears if its score exceeds the 95th percentile of the synthetic scores.
4. SelectselectRetain every bin that cleared, ranked by evidence. There is no top-k cut and no secondary filter.
5. ForecastforecastCombine the cleared conditions active on the latest bar and compare with their historical joint rate.

The synthetic markets. Each simulated day is drawn independently, with the observed average and co-movement of its open, close, high and low. A simulated day therefore looks like a real one, but carries no information about the next. Outside data used by a condition (such as bond yields) is held fixed, so only the market’s own history is randomized.

The full numerical specification — notation, worked examples and the decision rule — is published as a mathematical document.

4Limitations

  • A simple null. Synthetic days are drawn independently from a normal distribution. Real markets have volatility that clusters and heavier tails, so this null is easier to beat than reality; a condition that clears it has passed a necessary test, not a sufficient one.
  • No multiple-testing correction in the decision rule. A bin clears at a raw p-value below 0.05. Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted values are reported alongside, but do not change the decision; with many bins tested, some will clear by chance.
  • Resolution. With 1,000 synthetic markets, estimated p-values move in steps of 0.001.
  • Scope. Findings hold for the data and period of the workspace that produced them. AlphaVerify is research software, not investment advice.

5Using it

Install from the Python Package Index and run the shipped NASDAQ workspace. PyTorch is required; a CUDA-capable GPU is strongly recommended. Workspaces for bitcoin (daily and hourly) are included as well.

python -m pip install alphaverify

alphaverify init --workspace nasdaq_daily
alphaverify measure
alphaverify compare
alphaverify validate
alphaverify select
alphaverify forecast

After validate, the figure for every tested bin is written to workspaces/nasdaq_daily/03_validation/plot/.
