Open-source signal validation

AlphaVerify is an open-source framework for one question every trading signal should face: does its apparent edge hold up against markets that contain no signal at all?

The question

An edge, or something any market would produce?

Market folklore is full of conditions said to change the odds: a golden cross, price above its 200-day average, unusually low bond yields. Measured on history, many of them do appear to shift the probability of a rise or a fall. The question is whether that shift is an edge or something any market would produce by chance.

AlphaVerify answers it by running the same measurement on a thousand synthetic markets built to have no memory — markets in which, by construction, nothing in the past can predict the next day. A condition counts only if its real score beats 95% of the scores those signal-free markets produce.

Methodology

Five stages, each written to disk

Each condition is split into ranges (“bins”), and each bin passes through five stages. Selection consumes only a current validation result, and the forecast only a current selection.

The methodology in full
StageCommandWhat it does
1. MeasuremeasureFor each bin, the probability that the market moves by a given percentage within a given number of days.
2. ComparecompareSubtract the unconditional market baseline, leaving the shift the condition is associated with.
3. ValidatevalidateScore the shift and repeat the whole measurement on 1,000 synthetic markets. The bin clears if fewer than 5% of them score as high.
4. SelectselectKeep every bin that cleared, ranked by evidence, with adjusted values for how many would clear by chance.
5. ForecastforecastCombine the kept conditions active on the latest bar, and compare with their historical joint rate.
Limitations

What clearing does and does not mean

A memoryless null
Synthetic days are drawn independently, so the null has no volatility clustering or regimes. Clearing means a condition beats a market without memory — a necessary test, not proof of a usable edge.
Many tests
A bin clears at a raw p-value below 0.05, without multiple-testing correction. Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted values are reported for context; with many bins tested, some will clear by chance.
Scope
Findings hold for the data and period of the workspace that produced them. AlphaVerify is research software, not investment advice.

The method states every limitation in full.

Using it

Install and run a workspace

PyTorch is required; a CUDA-capable GPU is strongly recommended. Three workspaces ship with the package: NASDAQ daily, bitcoin daily and bitcoin hourly.

python -m pip install alphaverify

alphaverify init --workspace nasdaq_daily
alphaverify measure
alphaverify compare
alphaverify validate
alphaverify select
alphaverify forecast

After validate, the figure for every tested bin is written to workspaces/nasdaq_daily/03_validation/plot/.
