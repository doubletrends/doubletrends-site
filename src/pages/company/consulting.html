Capabilities

Areas of work

Data collection and pipelines

Automated collection from public web sources, APIs and documents, including JavaScript-rendered pages and PDF reports. Output is a validated dataset — schema-documented, typed and de-duplicated — as CSV, JSON or a direct database write. Ongoing collection includes scheduling, error logging and alerting. Work is limited to sources whose terms permit automated access.

Statistical analysis and forecasting

Exploratory analysis, hypothesis testing, regression, and time-series decomposition and forecasting in R or Python — including causal designs such as difference-in-differences and regression discontinuity, and the evaluation of experiments. Every result states its assumptions and what it can and cannot conclude.

Predictive models

Classification, regression, forecasting and anomaly detection on structured data: churn, demand, risk scoring, fraud and segmentation. Models are evaluated on held-out data and delivered with reproducible code and a model card covering performance by segment and known failure modes.

Case study · The firm’s own work

Building a point-in-time market regime classifier

The DoubleTrends Regime Indicator is the firm’s own system, and the clearest example of how engagements are built: the question, the data, the validation and the operations are all documented in public.

The question
Classify S&P 500 market stress every trading day from public data alone, with a back-tested history that avoids hindsight wherever the data allows, and a live system that publishes reliably after each close.
Data
Daily index prices, the VIX and three Federal Reserve series. Macroeconomic downloads are validated before use and retried on failure; if the source stays unavailable, the last validated copy (at most a week old) is used and the run is flagged.
Method
Measures of the depth, duration and sharpness of a decline, dual-horizon momentum and volatility percentiles feed a four-state classification and a signal state machine. The structure of every rule is published.
Validation
Thirty years of history are computed twice, independently — over the full series and date by date with only the data each date allowed. Both must agree before anything is published. A known limitation in the timing of macroeconomic inputs is disclosed rather than hidden.
Operations
Computation scheduled to the NYSE calendar, including early closes; retried dispatch and a backup end-of-day run; a monitoring view that flags missing or late readings; and a public revisions log.
Outputs
A methodology document, a complete signal record with a CSV download, and a daily reading delivered to subscribers.
Case study · The firm’s own work

An open-source validation framework, specified in full

AlphaVerify tests whether a trading signal’s edge survives comparison with markets that contain no signal — the kind of question research engagements exist to answer honestly.

The question
Does a market condition change the odds of a move by more than chance would? Answered for every range of every condition, across a grid of move sizes and horizons.
Method
Conditional move probabilities, baseline subtraction, and a null built from 1,000 synthetic markets with no memory. The full numerical specification is published.
Engineering
A staged command-line pipeline in Python and PyTorch with GPU support, written artifacts at every stage, an architecture covered by tests, and reproducible workspaces for the NASDAQ and bitcoin.
Distribution
Released on the Python Package Index under a public-domain (CC0) license, with its limitations stated alongside its findings.
