Principles

Four commitments govern how the indicator is built, published and described. They exist so that a reader can check the work rather than take it on trust.

Principles
  1. 1. Publish the rules
  2. 2. Publish the whole record
  3. 3. Answer one question well
  4. 4. Inform; do not instruct

1Publish the rules

The structure of every rule the indicator applies — what it reads, how it classifies, and when a signal may fire — is set out in the methodology. Parameter values are fixed and not published, but nothing about how a decision is reached is hidden behind a label such as “proprietary model”.

The rules do not change from one reading to the next, and there is no discretionary override: a reversal in a calm market is ignored however tempting it looks.

2Publish the whole record

Every signal is listed on the historical data page with what followed it, including the signals that came too early in long declines. A record that omits its failures is not a record.

History produced after the fact is labelled back-tested, and the way it was produced — including its known limitations — is documented. Live readings are recorded as they are published.

3Answer one question well

The indicator began as several separate measures, each a different view of when the market is stretched. Over time the work converged on a single question — is this a stressed market turning, or noise? — and one indicator that answers it.

The other measures were not discarded. They are explained in the research notes as the reasoning behind the indicator rather than sold as products of their own.

4Inform; do not instruct

The indicator describes market conditions. It does not know an investor’s circumstances and does not tell anyone what to do. The research is written so that readers understand why the market is classified as it is, and can reach their own decisions without depending on anyone’s call.
