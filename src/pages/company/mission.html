Principles

Four commitments govern how the indicator is built, published and described. They’re there so a reader can check the work instead of taking it on trust.

Principles
  1. 1. Publish the rules
  2. 2. Publish the whole record
  3. 3. Answer one question well
  4. 4. Inform; do not instruct

1Publish the rules

The structure of every rule the indicator applies — what it reads, how it classifies, and when a signal may fire — is set out in the methodology. Parameter values are fixed and kept private, but nothing about how a decision gets made is tucked behind a label like “proprietary model”.

The rules don’t change from one reading to the next and there is no discretionary override. A reversal in a calm market gets ignored, however tempting it looks.

2Publish the whole record

Every signal is listed on the historical data page with what followed it, including the signals that came too early in long declines. A record that leaves out its misses isn’t much of a record.

History produced after the fact is labelled back-tested, and how it was produced is documented, known limitations included. Live readings are recorded as they are published.

3Answer one question well

The indicator began as several separate measures, each a different view of when the market is stretched. Over time the work narrowed down to a single question — is this a stressed market turning, or noise? — and one indicator that answers it.

The other measures weren’t thrown out. They’re explained in the research notes as the reasoning behind the indicator instead of being sold as products on their own.

4Inform; do not instruct

The indicator describes market conditions. It knows nothing about an investor’s circumstances and doesn’t tell anyone what to do. The research is written so readers understand why the market is classified the way it is, and can make their own decisions without leaning on anyone’s call.
